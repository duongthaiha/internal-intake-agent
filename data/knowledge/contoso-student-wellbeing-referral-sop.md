## Purpose and scope

This SOP defines how concerns about a Contoso University student's wellbeing
are identified, referred, triaged, assigned, followed up, escalated, and closed.
It applies to self-referrals and referrals from staff, students, accommodation
teams, placements, or external partners. The Wellbeing Service is not an
emergency service and does not replace clinical, safeguarding, disability,
disciplinary, or emergency procedures.

## Roles and responsibilities

- The **Referrer** shares relevant observations, explains the referral where it
  is safe to do so, and responds to immediate danger.
- The **Wellbeing Triage Officer** reviews risk, urgency, consent, and service
  suitability.
- The **Case Coordinator** agrees support actions and maintains the case record.
- The **Safeguarding Lead** manages concerns involving abuse, exploitation,
  neglect, or risk to a child or adult at risk.
- **Academic Schools**, **Disability Support**, **Accommodation**, and external
  services provide support within their own responsibilities.

## Required information

A referral should contain the student's name and university identifier,
preferred contact method, factual observations, relevant dates, known immediate
risks, actions already taken, accessibility or communication needs, and the
student's consent status. Referrers should avoid diagnoses, speculation,
irrelevant personal history, or information gathered without a legitimate
reason.

## Procedure

1. **Address immediate danger.** If there is an immediate threat to life,
   serious injury, or an incident in progress, the referrer contacts emergency
   services and campus security before making a routine wellbeing referral. The
   student should not be left alone when doing so would increase risk.
2. **Discuss the referral.** Where safe and appropriate, the referrer explains
   the concern, the information to be shared, and the purpose of the Wellbeing
   Service, and seeks the student's consent. Lack of consent is recorded.
3. **Submit the referral.** The referrer or student uses the fictional Student
   Support Portal. The portal confirms receipt but does not promise a clinical
   outcome or immediate appointment.
4. **Triage the concern.** The Wellbeing Triage Officer checks urgency,
   safeguarding indicators, suicide or self-harm risk, risk to others,
   homelessness, domestic abuse, recent traumatic events, accessibility needs,
   and whether another service is better placed to respond.
5. **Select the response.** The outcome may be urgent contact, routine
   appointment, safeguarding review, welfare check, coordinated support,
   signposting, or return to the referrer for essential information.
6. **Contact the student.** The service uses the student's preferred safe
   contact method where known. Messages protect privacy and do not disclose
   sensitive details to another person answering the phone or viewing a shared
   account.
7. **Agree and coordinate support.** The Case Coordinator records the student's
   goals, agreed actions, responsible services, review dates, and any reasonable
   adjustments. Information is shared on a need-to-know basis.
8. **Follow up and close.** The coordinator reviews progress and attempts
   proportionate follow-up after missed contact. A case closes when actions are
   complete, the student declines further support, another service assumes
   responsibility, or repeated safe contact attempts are unsuccessful.

## Consent, confidentiality, and escalation

Consent is the normal basis for sharing a referral, but information may be
shared without consent when there is a credible serious risk, a safeguarding
obligation, or another lawful requirement. The decision, rationale, recipients,
and information shared must be recorded and limited to what is necessary.
Concerns about a child or adult at risk are escalated to the Safeguarding Lead.
An urgent mental health crisis is directed to emergency or local crisis
services. Staff must not promise absolute confidentiality or conduct their own
investigation into suspected abuse.

## Records and controls

The Student Support Portal is the authoritative wellbeing case record. Academic
records should contain only operational adjustments or outcomes needed for
education delivery, not detailed wellbeing notes. Access is role-based, and
records follow the fictional Student Support Retention Schedule. The service
reviews response times, accessibility, referral outcomes, repeat concerns, and
information-sharing decisions while reporting only appropriately aggregated
management information.
